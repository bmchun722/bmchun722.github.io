CIET · Surface Potential · Electric Double Layer · CO Reduction on Cu

Where Does the Interface Enter the Rate?

Two descriptions of the same electrode/electrolyte interface. Yoon and Bazant (2026) let the water at the reaction plane shift the effective electron concentration through a surface potential \(\Delta\chi_r\), constant within each group of metals. My microkinetic model of CO reduction on Cu lets the surface charge, set by the measured potential of zero charge, shift the energy of each adsorbate through its dipole. This page puts the two side by side and asks what each one misses.

1 · A constant surface potential per group of metals

Yoon and Bazant extend coupled ion-electron transfer (CIET) theory with two electrode properties: the intrinsic Fermi energy of the metal, \(\Delta\varepsilon_F\), and the surface potential at the reaction plane, \(\Delta\chi_r\), both relative to a Pt reference electrode (RHE). The reaction does not happen inside the metal but at a plane in the electrolyte, inside the double layer; the oriented water dipoles between the metal and that plane add a potential drop \(\chi_r\). Both terms enter through the excess electrochemical potential of the electrons (their Eq. 11):

$$\mu_e^{\mathrm{ex}} = \varepsilon + e\Delta\phi_e - e\Delta\chi_r = \varepsilon + \Delta\varepsilon_F - e\Delta\chi_r$$ $$\tilde c_e = \exp\!\left[\frac{\mu_e - \mu_e^{\mathrm{ex}}}{k_BT}\right] = \tilde c_{e,\infty}\,\exp\!\left[\frac{e\Delta\chi_r}{k_BT}\right] \qquad \text{(Eq. 12)}$$

So \(\Delta\chi_r\) acts on the effective concentration of reactive electrons, not on the energy of any adsorbate. When ion transfer limits the rate (the ICET regime), CIET reduces to Butler-Volmer kinetics, and the exchange current picks up the factor \((1-\alpha)\). With \(\Delta\varepsilon_F \approx -\Delta\Phi\) (work function, their Appendix B):

$$I_{0,\mathrm{ICET}} = I_0^*\, f_v(\tilde c_O, \tilde c_R)\, \exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta G_\ddagger^{\mathrm{ICET}} + (1-\alpha)(\Delta\varepsilon_F - e\Delta\chi_r)}{k_BT}\right) \quad \text{(Eq. 14)}$$ $$J_{0,\mathrm{ICET}} = J_0^* \exp\!\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)(\Delta\Phi + e\Delta\chi_r)}{k_BT}\right) \;\Rightarrow\; \log_{10} J_0 = \frac{1-\alpha}{k_BT\ln 10}\,(\Delta\Phi + e\Delta\chi_r) + \text{const} \quad \text{(Eq. 29)}$$

The slope of \(\log_{10}J_0\) against the descriptor is therefore \((1-\alpha)/(k_BT\ln 10)\). At 298 K, \(k_BT\ln 10 = 0.0592\) eV, so the fitted slope of 6.5 to 6.6 per eV corresponds to \(\alpha \approx 0.61\), as stated in the paper. Trasatti's HER data fall on two parallel lines: d metals with positively charged sp metals, and negatively charged sp metals (whether \(E_{\mathrm{eq,HER}}\) lies above or below \(E_{\mathrm{pzc}}\) decides the group). Yoon and Bazant set \(\Delta\chi_r = 0\) V for the first group and a single constant \(\Delta\chi_r = -0.4\) V for the second, and the two lines collapse. They state that a first-principles calculation of \(\Delta\chi_r\) for each interface is beyond the scope of their work. In Fig. 2a, Cu sits on the lower line, in the negatively charged sp group (\(\Delta\chi_r = -0.4\) V).

2 · Two pictures of the same interface

My model (CO reduction on Cu(211), pH 13, potentials vs. SHE) uses the double layer correction of Ringe (2023). The electrode is a plate capacitor whose charge follows from the measured potential of zero charge, and each adsorbate's formation energy shifts with that charge through \(\Delta a_i\), which is proportional to the change of its dipole moment on adsorption:

$$\sigma = C_{\mathrm{gap}}\,(U - U_{\mathrm{PZC}}), \qquad C_{\mathrm{gap}} = 20\ \mu\mathrm{F\,cm^{-2}},\quad U_{\mathrm{PZC}} = 0.09\ \mathrm{V}$$ $$\Delta E_i(\sigma) = \Delta E_i^0 + \Delta a_i\,\sigma$$

\(\Delta a_i\) comes from charged-slab DFT with implicit solvation (VASPsol: a linear dielectric, \(\varepsilon_b = 78.4\), with a linearized Poisson-Boltzmann electrolyte); the quadratic term of the fit is dropped. Move the potential and compare the two pictures.

The gap. The two terms are not the same quantity, and neither model contains the other. An implicit solvent (a linear dielectric) can give each adsorbate's response to the field, but the potential drop from water whose orientation depends on the sign of the surface charge needs an explicit picture of interfacial water (for example ab initio molecular dynamics). Bringing both into CIET means asking which part of the potential dependence of a step comes from the adsorbate (\(\Delta a\,\sigma\)) and which from the electrons at the reaction plane (\(\Delta\chi_r\)).
3 · HER is multistep too. What is different?

HER has its own elementary steps: Volmer, Heyrovsky and Tafel. Yoon and Bazant state that they assume the ICET mechanism for HER and fit exchange currents across metals with one rate law. The HER fit lumps the volcano function \(f_v\) into \(J_0^*\) (Eq. 30); Appendix D shows, for a generic adsorbed couple, how \(f_v\) gives a volcano when adsorption is fast. The paper argues that the cross-metal HER data can be understood without a unique hydrogen adsorption free energy for each electrode. It does not say which of the Volmer or Heyrovsky steps the ICET step is. Butler's 1936 derivation of the BV equation for HER assumed a fast Tafel step and a slow Volmer step. Click a step.

So "multistep" alone does not separate the two. What separates them is a network: branches that compete for the same CO*, chemical steps whose potential dependence comes only through the field, and rate control that moves from one part of the network to another with potential.

4 · Open questions
  1. Steps 14 and 15 control the C₂₊ rate at low overpotential and involve proton transfer from water. Do they stay in the ICET (Butler-Volmer-like) regime, or does any reach the regime limited by electron transfer?
  2. With \(U_{\mathrm{PZC}} = 0.09\) V, the Cu surface carries about \(-28\ \mu\mathrm{C\,cm^{-2}}\) at \(-1.3\) V. Is a single \(\Delta\chi_r\) for "negatively charged" surfaces enough at this charge, or does \(\Delta\chi_r\) depend on \(\sigma\)?
  3. How much of the potential dependence of a PCET step belongs to \(\Delta a\,\sigma\) and how much to \(\Delta\chi_r\)? Would adding both count the same water twice?
  4. What does an explicit water calculation (orientation at the reaction plane) add that implicit solvation cannot, and can its result be passed to a microkinetic model as one number per potential?
  5. Chemical steps such as 2CO* → OCCO* have no electron transfer. CIET does not apply to them, but their equilibrium shifts with \(\sigma\). How should a CIET network treat them consistently?
